\subsection{Tensor networks}

Tensors are multi-dimensional arrays of numbers that generalize the concept of scalars, vectors, and matrices. More than mere arrays, they are the natural mathematical objects for describing multilinear relationships, and their components transform in a prescribed way under a change of basis. Examples range from the stress tensor in continuum mechanics, differential forms in differential geometry, to density operators in quantum many-body systems.

To circumvent the curse of dimensionality, tensor networks represent or approximate a high-order tensor by contracting a collection of low-order tensors. The contraction pattern is encoded by a graph, the \emph{network}, whose vertices carry the tensors and whose edges denote the indices that are summed over; the remaining open edges correspond to the indices of the original tensor.

One famous example of a tensor network is the matrix product state (MPS), also known as the tensor train in numerical analysis [cite].
